# The bad recday: `ly05_20250620_20250623`

**Written 2026-10-08.** What is wrong with it, exactly, and what it does and does not affect.
Primary source: `docs/BUG_ly05_recday_mismatch.md` §11 (2026-10-02). Everything else below was
measured here.

---

## In one sentence

For this recday — and no other — the spike times in the **v1** pickle are aligned to the wrong
day's synchronisation pulses, so the neural data is shifted against the behaviour by between
**−9.98 and +38.58 seconds**, session by session.

## The fault, step by step

1. **An earlier, separate bug paired this mouse's recordings by position rather than by date.**
   That is the subject of `docs/BUG_ly05_recday_mismatch.md`: `ly05_20250618_20250619` and
   `ly05_20250620_20250623` were crossed.

2. **The 2026-09-01 fix repaired the neural arrays but not the sync caches.** It re-extracted
   `Neuron_raw` by date. It did not regenerate the untracked legacy rsync caches in
   `data/processed_data/ephys_rsync_mingyutest/`, which still carry the position-based pairing.

3. **So all 8 caches for this recday hold another recording's pulse train.** Each one named for a
   06-20/23 session is actually the raw ProbeB TTL train of the 06-18/19 recording *in the same
   session slot*, and matches every one of its pulses at offset 0:

   | session | cache named for | is really the train of | pulses |
   |---|---|---|---|
   | s0 | 2025-06-20 11-00-38 | 2025-06-18 12-18-16 | 483/483 |
   | s1 | 2025-06-20 12-58-31 | 2025-06-18 13-34-06 | 488/488 |
   | s2 | 2025-06-20 14-33-00 | 2025-06-18 14-52-48 | 525/525 |
   | s3 | 2025-06-20 15-51-57 | 2025-06-18 16-12-49 | 481/481 |
   | s4 | 2025-06-23 12-38-01 | 2025-06-19 12-28-25 | 461/461 |
   | s5 | 2025-06-23 13-51-50 | 2025-06-19 13-46-13 | 494/494 |
   | s6 | 2025-06-23 15-31-55 | 2025-06-19 15-41-28 | 516/516 |
   | s7 | 2025-06-23 16-45-40 | 2025-06-19 16-57-48 | 507/507 |

4. **`extract_neuron_raw.py --sync legacy` anchors each session on the cache's first pulse.** With
   the wrong cache, the correct spikes are placed against another day's clock origin.

5. **The result is a per-session shift.** Spikes are late by, for s0 to s7:

   | s0 | s1 | s2 | s3 | s4 | s5 | s6 | s7 |
   |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
   | +5.33 s | +2.55 s | +10.37 s | +10.26 s | −9.98 s | +38.58 s | +8.20 s | +5.71 s |

   Independently confirmed by behaviour: the reward PSTH peaks at +5.5, +10.5 and −9.5 s in the
   sessions where that is testable, instead of at 0.

**This is not the same bug as the v2/v3 OpenEphys counter-restart fault** (42 sessions, 0.94 to
29.16 s, `docs/handoff/SPIKE_CLOCK_V4.md`). That one never reached any El-Gaby result, because
those pickles were never used here. This one is specific to v1, which *is* what the published
LEC results were built on.

## Why it matters

At 25 ms bins the shifts are 102 to 1543 bins. Every quantity in this pipeline is locked to
`Trial_times`, so for the affected sessions the analysis reads spike counts from the wrong part
of the session. State tuning, goal-progress tuning and the anchoring are all computed from the
wrong window. It is not added noise; it is the wrong data.

**5 of the recday's 8 sessions reach the analysis**: s0, s1, s2, s4, s5 are exported; s3, s6 and
s7 have zero completed trials and are gated out. Of those five, four (s0, s1, s4, s5) are used as
cross-validation folds. The recday carries **91 of the 2851 LEC neurons**.

## How it shows in the data

Three independent fingerprints, all pointing the same way:

| measure | this recday | cohort |
|---|---|---|
| state-tuned neurons | **19 %** | 50 % |
| anchored fraction | **14.3 %** | median 58 %, range 14.3–70.7 % |
| co-anchored pairs in any sleep stage | **none**, so Figure 7 cell 87 drops it | every other recday contributes |

It is the minimum of all 25 recdays on anchoring. That is what destroyed tuning looks like, and
it is the reason this was findable at all.

## What it actually changes

Little, because the damage is self-limiting: tuning collapses, so the recday contributes few
neurons to any tuned pool. Removing it entirely:

| statistic | with | without | change |
|---|---|---|---|
| Figure 5, all state-tuned, Poisson | t 35.40 | t 35.97 | +0.57 |
| Figure 5, non-zero-lag 30°, Poisson | t 9.91 | t 9.95 | +0.04 |
| Figure 5, non-zero-lag 90°, Poisson | t 2.15 | t 2.08 | −0.06 |
| Figure 5, all state-tuned, ElasticNet | t 33.31 | t 33.95 | +0.65 |
| anchoring coherent proportion | 0.4615 | 0.4637 | +0.0022 |
| Figure 7 | — | — | nil, already dropped |

No conclusion depends on it, and every change is in the favourable direction. It contributes 17
of the 1426 state-tuned neurons and 17 of the 1426 neurons in the anchoring statistic.

## Status

* **Fixed in v4.** `data_dic_lec_v4.pkl` (2026-10-02) pairs each mouse's own probe stream with its
  own pyControl train by pairwise offset, so the legacy caches are not used at all.
* **v1 is left as is**, by user decision of 2026-10-01. Any result built on `data_dic_lec.pkl`
  contains this.
* **The El-Gaby LEC run is migrating to v4** (`mFC_schema-main_unblocked_lec_v4/`,
  `lec_replication_run_v4/`, submitted 2026-10-08). The v1 results stay in
  `lec_replication_run/` for comparison.

## What is not established

* Whether the recday's tuning recovers under v4. That is the single clearest test of the fix and
  the v4 run will answer it: expect the state-tuned fraction to rise from 19 % toward 50 % and the
  anchored fraction from 14.3 % toward the 58 % median. **A large move here is the expected
  result; a large move in any other recday would need explaining.**
* Whether any other recday has a quieter version of the same cache fault. The audit of all 195
  legacy caches says no — all except these 8 come from their own recording — but the smaller
  per-mouse offsets it also found (ah08 −121 to +38 ms, ly06 +61 to +171 ms, ly05 elsewhere 0.1 to
  0.46 s) are real and are in v1 too.

## How to verify it yourself

The cache check from `BUG_ly05_recday_mismatch.md` §11: take each cache's pulse train and
histogram its pairwise differences, at 1 ms, against every TTL stream of every June recording.
A cache's true source is the stream whose peak holds every pulse.

For the downstream consequence, the per-session v1-versus-v4 wall-clock comparison is in
`data/processed_data/alignment_qc_lec_tv3_rmingyutest.v2.csv` against
`alignment_qc_lec_nv4_tv3_rv4.v2.csv`. Beware
`data/processed_data/neuron_raw_v4_vs_legacy_lec.csv`: its lag column is computed with
`max_lag = 40` bins (±1 s), so this recday's values are pinned at the window edge and understate
the error. A lag search must be wider than the error it looks for.

## 2026-10-08: the clock is confirmed fixed in v4; the tuning question stays open

Checked directly in the alignment QC tables rather than inferred from the cache audit. Two files,
same recday, same acceptance window (−6.14 s to +2.54 s, set by the cohort):

| session | v1 offset (s) | v1 OK | v1 reward peak (s) | v4 offset (s) | v4 OK | v4 reward peak (s) |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| s0 | +4.07 | no | +5.50 | −1.26 | yes | 0.00 |
| s1 | +1.46 | yes | — | −1.09 | yes | — |
| s2 | +9.00 | no | +10.50 | −1.37 | yes | 0.00 |
| s3 | +9.41 | no | — | −0.85 | yes | — |
| s4 | −11.87 | no | −9.50 | −1.90 | yes | 0.00 |
| s5 | +36.21 | no | −12.50 | −2.37 | yes | +0.25 |
| s6 | +5.81 | no | — | −2.39 | yes | — |
| s7 | +3.29 | no | — | −2.42 | yes | — |

v1 = `alignment_qc_lec_tv3_rmingyutest.csv` (the legacy `ephys_rsync_mingyutest` caches).
v4 = `alignment_qc_lec_nv4_tv3_rv4.csv`. v1 fails the window in 7 of 8 sessions; v4 passes in 8 of
8. Every v1 reward PSTH peaks seconds from zero; every v4 one peaks in the 0 bin except s5 at one
250 ms bin. The reward PSTH is an independent read — it uses the behavioural reward times, not the
sync pulses that define the offset — so the two agree by measurement, not by construction.

Note the two offset conventions. The step-by-step section above quotes the error against v4
(+5.33, +2.55, +10.37, +10.26, −9.98, +38.58, +8.20, +5.71 s); the QC table quotes each version's
own wall-clock metric against the common reference. They differ by exactly v4's own baseline
(−1.26 to −2.42 s) and are the same measurement.

An intermediate state is worth recording because it is a trap: `alignment_qc_lec_nv2_tv3.csv`
(neuron v2, non-legacy rsync) still fails in 4 of 8 sessions, with a *different* residual pattern
(s0 +3.05, s3 +10.65, s4 +3.92, s7 +19.71). Moving off the legacy caches is necessary but was not
sufficient; only the v4 fitted clock clears the recday. Any analysis built on the v2 or v3 pickle
carries its own version of this fault, not the one documented above.

Still open: whether the **tuning** recovers. A correct clock is a precondition, not a guarantee —
the recday has only 3 sessions with >2 trials and could stay a weak recday for behavioural
reasons. The v4 El-Gaby chain is the test, and the prediction in the section above stands
unchanged.
